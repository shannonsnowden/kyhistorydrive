# Tribal review packet: territory layer

Prepared by KY History (content/research) for issue #145. This is a reviewer packet only; no geometry was changed. The review itself needs Shannon to choose reviewers and send outreach.

How to use: each section lists what the map currently claims (years, confidence, basis) and its sources, so a reviewer can check one nation or culture without reading code. Archaeological cultures are marked; they are not tribes and the site makes no descent claim for them.

## Cherokee
- Type: Nation
- File: `/data/web/territories/cherokee-treaty-lines-and-chickamauga.json`

## Six Nations (Haudenosaunee)
- Type: Nation
- File: `/data/web/territories/six-nations-fort-stanwix-line-of-property.json`
- Years shown: 1768 to 1784
- Confidence: low-medium
- presenceLabel: Six Nations cession or claim in this area (contested)
- basis: Treaty text (Stanwix 1768 deed and proceedings) plus the Ohio River centreline (Natural Earth). County list is a clip of the deed’s Ohio River call, not a surveyed boundary. The Susquehanna-Delaware-Wood Creek legs are outside the map window and are described, not drawn. Native Land Digital data was not used.
- overlapNote: The whole Kentucky cession overlaps the Cherokee (Hard Labour 14 Oct 1768 and Lochaber 1770 drew Cherokee lines in the same land; Johnson wrote that he never found the Cherokee claiming north of the Tennessee, while Stuart complained), the Shawnee (present but not signers; contested until Camp Charlotte 1774) and the Chickasaw in the far west. The Board of Trade had told Johnson to stop at the Kanawha; the Six Nations insisted on the Tennessee. The Lenape and Shawnee were named in the deed as dependents, which they denied.
