Christopher Gist, 1750–51

Fifteen-plus years before Boone's fame, the Ohio Company hired surveyor Christopher Gist to probe the Ohio Valley down toward the Falls.

Crossing into Kentucky from the Scioto mouth, his journal logged rich bottomlands, canebrakes, buffalo plains, and Native towns. Shawnee at Lower Shawneetown warned him off the Falls, where French-allied Indians camped. He brought the company a fossil "beest" tooth from Big Bone country and the first detailed English read of northeastern Kentucky.

View the map

Christopher Gist set out from Cresap's Old Town on the Potomac on October 31, 1750, went by Shannopin's Town and Logstown to the Wyandot town on the Muskingum, and on to Lower Shawneetown at the mouth of the Scioto, with a side trip to Pickawillany on the Great Miami. On March 12, 1751 he crossed into Kentucky and went down the south side of the Ohio to a salt lick said to be 15 miles above the Falls, then turned south across the Red River, which he called the little Cuttaway, and struggled east through the mountains to the Bluestone and the New River, reaching his home on the Yadkin on May 18.

About 350 miles from Old Town to Lower Shawneetown, 250 for the Pickawillany round trip, and about 480 from Lower Shawneetown through Kentucky to the Yadkin, measured on the map line.

Approximate stops, in order of travel

  1. Oldtown, Allegany County, Maryland (Col. Thomas Cresap's at Old Town)
  2. Bedford to Ligonier area, Bedford and Westmoreland counties, Pennsylvania (Juniata, Allegheny Mountain, and Loyalhanna)
  3. Pittsburgh and Ambridge/Baden, Allegheny and Beaver counties, Pennsylvania (Shannopin's Town and Logstown)
  4. Coshocton, Coshocton County, Ohio (Wyandot town on the Muskingum)
  5. Lancaster and the Circleville area, Fairfield and Pickaway counties, Ohio (Hockhocking and Maguck)
  6. Portsmouth, Ohio / South Portsmouth, Kentucky, Scioto County, Ohio, and Greenup County, Kentucky (Lower Shawneetown)
  7. Piqua, Miami County, Ohio (Pickawillany)
  8. South Portsmouth, Greenup County, Kentucky (ferried over the Ohio)
  9. Lewis, Mason, and Bracken counties, Kentucky (down the southeast side of the Ohio, conjectural)
  10. near Grant and Owen counties, Kentucky (lower Salt Lick Creek, disputed, conjectural)
  11. Red River near Clay City, Clark, Powell, and Estill counties, Kentucky (little Cuttaway (Red River), conjectural)
  12. Bluegrass edge to the eastern Kentucky coalfield, Clark and Powell counties to Letcher County, Kentucky, and Wise County, Virginia (southeast through the mountains, conjectural)
  13. Bluefield–Princeton area, Mercer County, West Virginia, and Tazewell County, Virginia (Bluestone River, conjectural)
  14. near Hinton, Summers County, West Virginia (New River, conjectural)
  15. Mountain Lake, Giles County, Virginia (mountain pond and Sinking Creek, conjectural)
  16. near Blacksburg, Montgomery County, Virginia (Richard Hall's, conjectural)
  17. upper Yadkin, Surry, Yadkin, and Davie counties, North Carolina (home on the Yadkin, conjectural)

Sources: Gist's journal (Darlington 1893; Summers 1929).
