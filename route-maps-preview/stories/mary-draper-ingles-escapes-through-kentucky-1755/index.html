Mary Draper Ingles escapes through Kentucky, 1755

In July 1755 a Shawnee war party struck Draper's Meadow on the Virginia frontier and carried Mary Draper Ingles, then about twenty-three and pregnant, west with her two young sons.

Photos

Big Bone Lick State Park entrance sign, Boone County

Photo: Mattguyver. Wikimedia Commons (opens in a new tab). License: CC BY 3.0.

After the long march to the Ohio villages she was put to work at a salt-making camp at Big Bone Lick in what is now Boone County—among the earliest recorded European women on Kentucky ground.

Around mid-October she slipped away with an older Dutch captive woman and walked the Ohio, Kanawha, and New River corridors for roughly forty-two days, foraging on walnuts, grapes, and roots until she reached a New River cabin near her old home. Her son Thomas was later ransomed; she lived until 1815. The Kentucky Historical Society marker at Big Bone Lick still marks the breakout that turned a captivity story into one of the frontier's great return journeys.
