    1. Big Bone Lick State Historic Site

    PublicLimited hoursFossils sent to Jefferson 1807. State historic site; museum hours vary.

    Ice Age mammoths, mastodons, bison and other animals came to the salt springs here; in 1807 Thomas Jefferson asked George Rogers Clark to have bones collected at the lick shipped east. Sources: Grokipedia: Big Bone Lick State Park

    3. Dinsmore Homestead

    PublicPaid admissionLimited hoursHouse 1842. Historic house; tours seasonal, admission charged.

    The Dinsmore family settled here in 1842 and, with the help of slave labor, raised sheep and grew grapes and willows for a basket business run by German immigrants; the house keeps its original furnishings. Sources: Wikipedia: Dinsmore Homestead

    4. Battery Hooper Park (Fort Wright)

    PublicBuilt 1862. City park at 1402 Highland Pike; open daily.

    Hooper Battery was a hilltop artillery earthwork built in 1862, one of the Union defenses of Cincinnati raised in Northern Kentucky when Confederate forces advanced toward the city. The restored battery is now the centerpiece of a city park. Sources: City of Fort Wright: Battery Hooper Park history; Wikipedia: Hooper Battery

    5. John A. Roebling Suspension Bridge

    PublicOpened 1866. Open to traffic and pedestrians.

    John A. Roebling's bridge between Covington and Cincinnati; the last cable wire crossed on June 23, 1866. Its main span was briefly the world's longest, before his Brooklyn Bridge. Sources: Grokipedia: John A. Roebling Suspension Bridge
