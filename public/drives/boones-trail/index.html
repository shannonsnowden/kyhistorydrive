Boone's Trail and the Wilderness Road

From Cumberland Gap, where Thomas Walker followed an Indian road in 1750, north along the settlers' route to Logan's and Whitley's stations and Boone's fort on the Kentucky River, then east to Pilot Knob, by tradition where Boone's party first looked out over Kentucky in 1769.

    1. Cumberland Gap National Historical Park

    Walker 1750; Boone 1775

    Visiting: Public National Park Service site; free.

    The natural gap in the Appalachians where Kentucky, Tennessee and Virginia meet. Dr. Thomas Walker recorded it in April 1750, following an Indian road; Daniel Boone's frontiersmen opened the path, and settlers on the Wilderness Road later passed through it. Sources: Grokipedia: Cumberland Gap National Historical Park; NPS: Cumberland Gap; Wikipedia: Cumberland Gap; Grokipedia: Thomas Walker (explorer)

    3. William Whitley House State Historic Site

    Built 1787–1794

    Visiting: PublicLimited hours Historic site; seasonal hours.

    William and Esther Whitley's home near Crab Orchard, built as a fortress against attack between 1787 and 1794; often called Kentucky's first brick house. The grounds include Sportsman's Hill, an early racetrack. Sources: Wikipedia: William Whitley House State Historic Site

    4. Logan's Station / St. Asaph (Stanford)

    Built 1775

    Visiting: PublicMarker only Town park and marker area in Stanford; no reconstructed fort.

    In 1775 Benjamin Logan left Boone's party and built Logan's Station (St. Asaph) by Buffalo Spring near present Stanford. Logan later helped secure Kentucky statehood. Sources: Wikipedia: Benjamin Logan; Grokipedia: Stanford, Kentucky

    5. Fort Boonesborough State Park

    Founded April 1775

    Visiting: PublicPaid admissionLimited hours State park; fort exhibits have seasonal hours and admission.

    Daniel Boone's party founded Boonesborough after crossing the Kentucky River in April 1775; the settlement was a major frontier outpost during the Revolution. The fort site is a National Historic Landmark. Sources: Grokipedia: Fort Boonesborough State Park

    6. Pilot Knob State Nature Preserve (Boone's Overlook)

    June 7, 1769

    Visiting: PublicLimited hours State nature preserve; free, open daylight hours. Small gravel lot at the Brush Creek Road trailhead, north of Clay City. The overlook is a strenuous 1.25-mile climb each way, with unrailed cliffs at the top. Stay on the trail.

    John Filson's 1784 Boone narrative says that on June 7, 1769, the party "found ourselves on Red river, where John Finley had formerly been trading with the Indians, and, from the top of an eminence, saw with pleasure the beautiful level of Kentucky." By tradition, recorded on state historical marker 132, that eminence is Pilot Knob. Finley, a trader, had guided Daniel Boone's party from North Carolina. Sources: Office of Kentucky Nature Preserves: Pilot Knob SNP; Kentucky Historical Society: Marker 132, Pilot Knob; Collins, History of Kentucky (Madison Co.), quoting Filson
