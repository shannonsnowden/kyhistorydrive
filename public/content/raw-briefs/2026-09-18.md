🤠 Maj. Silas Harlan
Virginia-born Major Silas Harlan (c. 1752–1782) came to Kentucky in 1774, built Harlan's Station on the Salt River about seven miles south of Harrodsburg in 1778, and commanded spies in George Rogers Clark's Illinois campaign. Clark later said he was "one of bravest soldiers that ever fought by my side." Harlan fell leading an advance party at Blue Licks on August 19, 1782, and was buried on the field; Harlan County, created in 1819, carries his name though he never lived in those mountains.

🏙 Glasgow & the Barrens
Barren County was authorized December 20, 1798, from Green and Warren, named for the open "barrens" prairie. In 1799 settlers chose John Gorin's Big Spring site over Richard Garnett's Beaver Creek spring for the county seat; Gorin opened a tavern in his cabin that year and put land and cash toward public buildings on a pea-vine hill that became Glasgow's square. The legislature recognized the town in 1809; the spring's steady water made the square possible.
Source: https://history.ky.gov/markers/barren-county-1798

🪶 Chickamauga Cherokee
After the 1775 Treaty of Sycamore Shoals, war chief Dragging Canoe broke from Overhill leaders who had sold vast hunting grounds and led followers down the Tennessee to new towns on Chickamauga Creek (winter 1776–77). American settlers called them Chickamaugas. From those towns—and later the Five Lower Towns—they kept up uncompromising resistance, raiding with Northwestern Confederacy allies as far as Kentucky, Indiana, and Virginia until the 1794 Tellico Blockhouse peace.
Source: https://grokipedia.com/page/Chickamauga_Cherokee

🏺 Chiggerville (15Oh1)
On the Green River floodplain in Ohio County, the Late Archaic Chiggerville shell midden (roughly 3000–1000 BC) sits beside an old mussel shoal and runs more than four feet thick across a 170-by-350-foot spread. University of Kentucky digs in 1938 and 2009 recovered thousands of artifacts plus 114 human burials, 17 dog burials, and dense hearths and pits—freshwater mussels, drum and catfish, turtles, acorns and hickory, and Saratoga-cluster points with distinctive Green River humped bannerstones.
Source: https://archaeology.ky.gov/Find-a-Site/Pages/Chiggerville.aspx

🕳️ Salts Cave (15Ht4)
Inside Mammoth Cave National Park, Salts Cave was one of several passages Early Woodland people used most intensively about 1000–200 BC. They mined wall sulfates—gypsum for pigment, mirabilite as medicine and seasoning—leaving shell scrapers, hammerstones, and battered ledges, and carried cane torches, textile slippers, baskets, and gourd containers deep underground. After about 200 BC that mining and exploration faded as beliefs and practices shifted.
Source: https://archaeology.ky.gov/Find-a-Site/Pages/Mammoth-Cave.aspx
