# Kentucky History Morning Update — 2026-10-03

This morning's Kentucky history brief:

Salt River Stations, Blue Jacket, and the Old Reliable

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Saturday, October 3, 2026*

🤠 McAfee Station on the Salt River
In May 1773, Virginia’s McAfee Company—brothers James, Robert, George, Samuel, and William, with James McCoun Jr. and Samuel Adams—surveyed fertile Salt River bottomlands in today’s Mercer County. After Point Pleasant service and delayed returns, they built a stockade on James McAfee’s land in 1779; by 1781 nearly every local family sheltered there, and a Shawnee attack that May was beaten back with help from nearby stations. New Providence Presbyterian Church (1785) grew from the same community, and the modern hamlet of McAfee still marks the pioneer name.
Source: https://explorekyhistory.ky.gov/items/show/987

🏙️ Washington, Mason County
In 1785 Arthur Fox and Baptist preacher William Wood bought about 700 acres and laid out a one-mile-square town; Virginia chartered Washington in 1786—widely cited as the first American settlement named for George Washington while Kentucky was still a Virginia county. By the 1790 census it had 462 residents in 119 houses, second only to Lexington; it became Mason County’s seat in 1788, hosted an early regional post office, and later yielded the seat to riverside Maysville (1848) before Maysville annexed Old Washington in 1990.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_Kentucky

🪶 Blue Jacket (Weyapiersenwah)
Shawnee war chief Blue Jacket (c. 1743–1810) first appears in British records at his Scioto town in 1773, fought at Point Pleasant in Lord Dunmore’s War, and allied with the British through the Revolution. Benjamin Logan’s 1786 Kentucky militia expedition burned his Bellefontaine-area town; he negotiated with Kentuckians at Limestone (Maysville) in 1787, then raided back into Kentucky in 1788. With Miami chief Little Turtle he helped shatter Harmar’s and St. Clair’s armies (1790–91)—St. Clair’s Defeat remains the U.S. Army’s worst loss to Native forces—before defeat at Fallen Timbers (1794) and the Treaty of Greenville (1795) that opened most of Ohio to settlement and reshaped the Ohio–Kentucky frontier.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Jacket

🏛️ Slone Site (15Pi11), Pike County
On the north bank of the Levisa Fork of the Big Sandy, Slone was a late Middle Fort Ancient circular village occupied about 1350–1400 CE for roughly thirty to forty-five years. University of Kentucky crews excavated about eighty percent of the site in 1963–64 before Fishtrap Reservoir rose; they mapped at least twelve houses around a plaza, concentric cooking/storage and residential zones, family cemeteries behind the houses, and three successive wooden palisades that grew the enclosure from just over 200 to about 255 feet across. More than 150 stone discoidals—likely chunkey game pieces—underscore daily life in this eastern Kentucky Fort Ancient community.
Source: https://archaeology.ky.gov/Find-a-Site/Pages/Slone.aspx

🪨 Louisville & Nashville Railroad opens the interior
Kentucky chartered the Louisville & Nashville on March 5, 1850, to build south toward Nashville; Tennessee authorized the extension in 1851. Track laying began in Louisville in May 1853; the first eight-mile excursion ran August 25, 1855, and on October 27, 1859, the first train covered the full ~187-mile Louisville–Nashville main line. Unlike the earlier Lexington & Ohio Bluegrass short line, the L&N tied the Falls city to the Deep South, survived Civil War use by both sides, and later drove branch construction into western and eastern Kentucky coalfields—the “Old Reliable” that opened much of the commonwealth’s interior freight map.
Source: https://www.lnrr.org/History.aspx
