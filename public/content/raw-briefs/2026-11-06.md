# Blind Hero, Bush Settlement & Greensburg

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Friday, November 6, 2026*

🐴 **Horse series: Lexington, the Blind Hero of Woodburn**
Lexington was foaled on March 17, 1850, at The Meadows, the Fayette County farm of Dr. Elisha Warfield, and was first named Darley. In 1856 Robert A. Alexander of Woodburn Stud near Midway bought him for $15,000. At Woodburn he became known as the "Blind Hero of Woodburn." He led the North American sire list every year from 1861 through 1874, and died at Woodburn on July 1, 1875.

🏠 **Capt. Billy Bush and the Bush Settlement**
Capt. William "Billy" Bush organized the Bush Settlement on Lower Howard's Creek in what became Clark County. Beginning January 7, 1783, Daniel Boone, acting as deputy surveyor, ran the first eleven surveys for him. Baptist families began settling there in November 1784, and Providence Church became the center of the community.

🏛️ **Greensburg takes shape on the Green River**
On December 4, 1794, Walter Beal laid out about 100 acres into lots around a public square above a natural ford on the Green River, on the former site of Glover's Station. Green County had been formed in December 1792. A log courthouse went up in 1796, and the Old Stone Courthouse was finished in 1804.

🗺️ **When the Neutral fell, the hunting ground opened**
In the winter of 1650–51, Haudenosaunee war parties overran the country of the Neutral (Attawandaron) in what is now southern Ontario and ended the Neutral Confederacy as a separate nation. The upper Ohio Valley was left as contested hunting ground. These events took place in Ontario, so this story has no Kentucky map pin.

🏺 **Guilfoil Village (15Fa176), Fayette County**
Guilfoil Village is a Middle Fort Ancient village in Fayette County, recorded as site 15Fa176, with a period of significance of AD 1000 to 1499. It is a protected archaeological site on private land. The public pin is a rounded, county-level point.
