# Green Clay, Barbourville, Aristides, Cold Oak & the Catawba

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Friday, November 13, 2026*

🧭 **Gen. Green Clay, the surveyor who built Clermont**
Green Clay came to Kentucky as a young Virginia surveyor and was a deputy surveyor of Lincoln County by 1781. Taking part of his fees in land, he built holdings of more than 40,000 acres, ran Kentucky River ferries including Clay's Ferry, and completed Clermont in Madison County in 1799. In May 1813 he led Kentucky militia to the relief of Fort Meigs and later held the fort. His son Cassius remade Clermont as White Hall.

🏛️ **Barbourville, the Knox County seat on the Cumberland**
Knox County was created from Lincoln County on December 19, 1799. James Barbour gave 38 acres for its seat, and Barbourville was laid out in 1800 beside a Wilderness Road tavern; Thomas Walker's 1750 cabin site is nearby. On September 19, 1861, Home Guards fought Zollicoffer's Confederates at the town bridge in the first skirmish in eastern Kentucky.

🐎 **Horse series: Aristides, winner of the first Kentucky Derby**
Aristides was foaled in 1872 at H. Price McGrath's McGrathiana near Lexington, by Leamington out of Sarong, a daughter of Lexington. Entered to set the pace for his stablemate, he won the first Kentucky Derby on May 17, 1875, for Black trainer Ansel Williamson and 19-year-old jockey Oliver Lewis.

🪨 **Cold Oak Shelter, Lee County**
Cold Oak is a rock overhang in Lee County used seasonally at the end of the Late Archaic, about 1200 to 1000 BC. Storage pits held seeds of sunflower, goosefoot, knotweed, marshelder and squash, early crops of the Eastern Agricultural Complex. It is a protected archaeological site.

🗺️ **The Catawba and the northern war parties**
After the Great Peace of Montreal in 1701, Iroquois war parties turned south against the Catawba of the Carolina Piedmont, along warpaths that included the Warriors' Path through Cumberland Gap. In 1753 the Shawnee war leader The Pride died in a South Carolina jail on his way to raid the Catawba. These events took place outside Kentucky, so this story has no Kentucky map pin.
