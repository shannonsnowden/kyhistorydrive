# Fort Hartford, Fort McIntosh, and Yahoo Falls

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Wednesday, October 14, 2026*

### 🏙️ Hartford: Fort Hartford on the Rough River
Website swap for “Taylorsville at Brashears Creek” (already a story, taylorsville-on-brashears-creek, 2026-09-10). Hartford grew up on a bluff above the Rough River, on part of Gabriel Madison’s 4,000-acre Virginia grant surveyed in 1782; the state marker for Fort Hartford calls it the first fortified place in the lower Green River Valley. William Smeathers later wrote that he helped build “a fort at Hartford on Rough Creek” in 1782–83. Ohio County was created in December 1798, its first court met in 1799, and Hartford became the seat on land Madison gave the town. The log courthouse of 1799–1800 collapsed in 1813, the post office opened as Hartford Court House in 1801, and the town was incorporated in 1808. The name may come from a ford where deer crossed, but its origin is uncertain.
Source: [KHS marker #1195: Site of Fort Hartford](https://history.ky.gov/markers/site-of-fort-hartford) · [Hartford, Kentucky (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Kentucky)

### 🪶 Treaty of Fort McIntosh, 1785
On January 21, 1785, U.S. commissioners George Rogers Clark, Richard Butler, and Arthur Lee met Wyandot, Delaware, Chippewa, and Ottawa representatives at Fort McIntosh (today’s Beaver, Pennsylvania) and drew a reservation line through northwest Ohio. Lands east, south, and west of that line, including Shawnee country in southwestern Ohio opposite Kentucky, were declared U.S. territory. Most Ohio Country nations, especially the Shawnee, rejected a treaty their warriors had not authorized. The disputed Ohio River boundary helped spark the Northwest Indian War that kept Kentucky’s northern frontier under arms until Greenville in 1795.
Source: [Treaty of Fort McIntosh (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fort_McIntosh) · [Treaty text (Kappler, OSU)](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wyandot-etc-1785-0006)

### 🏛️ Chambers Site (15Ml109)
On a Marshall County ridgetop above the Middle Fork of Clarks River, Chambers is a roughly six-hectare Mississippian village excavated after a mid-1980s bridge-borrow discovery. David Pollack and Jimmy Railey documented wall-trench houses, a large trash midden, and thick prepared-floor deposits; radiocarbon dates cluster about A.D. 1250–1350 (Angelly phase). Residents raised maize, gathered nuts and maygrass, and used both Dover and Mill Creek chert hoes, an inland upland community away from the big Ohio–Mississippi mound centers.
Source: [The Archaeology of Kentucky: An Update, vol. 2 (Kentucky Heritage Council)](https://heritage.ky.gov/Documents/TheArchaeologyofKYAnUpdateVol2.pdf)

### 💧 Yahoo Falls
In McCreary County inside Big South Fork National River and Recreation Area, Yahoo Falls drops about 113 feet in a single plunge, widely billed as Kentucky’s tallest waterfall, into a sandstone amphitheater laced with rhododendron and mountain laurel. The short gorge trail also reaches Yahoo Arch. Forest Service recreation work in the 1960s opened the overlook and stairs so visitors could reach the falls without a scramble; the National Park Service has managed it since 1987.
