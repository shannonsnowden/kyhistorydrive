# Kentucky History Morning Update — 2026-09-23

Jack Jouett, Simpsonville & the Chickamauga Stand

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Wednesday, September 23, 2026*

🤠 Jack Jouett, Kentucky’s midnight rider
On the night of June 3–4, 1781, Capt. John “Jack” Jouett spotted Banastre Tarleton’s British cavalry near Cuckoo Tavern in Virginia and rode roughly forty miles over back roads to warn Thomas Jefferson at Monticello and the Virginia Assembly at Charlottesville. Most of the government escaped; Virginia rewarded him with a sword and pistols. By 1782 he had moved west into what became Kentucky, represented Lincoln/Mercer and later Woodford counties as a statehood backer, and in 1797 built a brick Federal house on a 530-acre Woodford farm near Craig’s Creek — still standing as the Jack Jouett House Historic Site.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Jouett

🏙️ Simpsonville takes shape on the Louisville road
In Shelby County, Isaac Watkins petitioned to lay out Simpsonville in 1816 as a waystation on the Louisville–Shelbyville road. A post office opened in 1821; the town incorporated in 1833. It was named for Capt. John Simpson, a Shelby County lawyer and Speaker of the Kentucky House (1810–11) who fought at Fallen Timbers (1794), won a seat in Congress in 1812, then died at the River Raisin on January 22, 1813. By the mid-1820s the place was a busy Midland Trail stage stop — home ground for anyone driving US 60 past Bagdad today.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpsonville,_Kentucky

🪶 Chickamauga Cherokee hold the southern flank
After the 1775 Treaty of Sycamore Shoals sold Cherokee claims to Kentucky hunting ground, Dragging Canoe (Tsiyu Gansini) broke with the accommodationist chiefs, warning of a “dark and bloody ground.” From new towns on Chickamauga Creek (and later the Five Lower Towns), his Chickamauga faction — Cherokees plus allied Creek, Shawnee, and others — raided Cumberland and Kentucky settlements through the Revolution and into the 1790s. Resistance ended with the 1794 Treaty of Tellico Blockhouse; Dragging Canoe himself died in 1792 at Running Water Town.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragging_Canoe

🗿 Tolu Site — Mississippian mounds on the lower Ohio
Near Tolu in Crittenden County, the Tolu Site (15Cn1) was a Mississippian town occupied mainly about 1200–1300 CE (pottery styles; occupation into the 1400s). It once held three mounds — a platform, a burial mound, and a third of unclear use — and belongs to the Angel phase “Kincaid Set” with Angel (IN), Kincaid (IL), and Wickliffe (KY). Webb and Funkhouser excavated it in 1930; a 1954 plow find west of town produced one of the finest Mississippian fluorite statues known, complete with a rare beaded forelock.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolu_Site

⛏️ Saltpeter Cave at Carter Caves
In Carter County’s Carter Caves State Resort Park, Saltpeter Cave (15Cr99) was mined for potassium nitrate during the War of 1812, when British blockades forced Americans to scrape gunpowder ingredients from their own caves. University of Kentucky archaeologists in the early 1990s documented impressions and remnants of 26 wooden leaching vats — a hybrid of big-box and small V-shaped cottage setups with wooden piping. Kentucky caves yielded more than 300,000 pounds of saltpeter statewide; this one is on the National Register and open for summer tours.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltpeter_Cave
