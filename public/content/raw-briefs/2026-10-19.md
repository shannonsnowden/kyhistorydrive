# Father Rice, La Grange, and the Maysville Road Veto

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Monday, October 19, 2026*

### 🤠 Father David Rice and Kentucky’s first Presbyterian churches
Father David Rice organized Concord Church at Danville in 1784, argued for gradual emancipation in 1792, and later preached from his home near Greensburg.
Source: [First Presbyterian Church of Danville: Our History](https://www.presbydan.org/our-history) · [ExploreKYHistory: Home of Early Minister (marker 844)](https://explorekyhistory.ky.gov/items/show/174)

### 🏙️ La Grange: the Oldham County seat with trains on Main Street
La Grange began in 1827 at a crossroads named for Lafayette’s estate, became the Oldham County seat in 1838, and still runs trains down Main Street.
Source: [Grokipedia: La Grange, Kentucky](https://grokipedia.com/page/La_Grange,_Kentucky) · [KHS marker #1251: Oldham County, 1824](https://history.ky.gov/markers/oldham-county-1824)

### 🪶 River Raisin, 1813: Kentucky’s winter defeat in Michigan
Kentucky volunteers who mustered at Georgetown in 1812 were defeated at the River Raisin in January 1813 by British troops and Wyandot-led warriors.
Source: [Grokipedia: Battle of Frenchtown](https://grokipedia.com/page/Battle_of_Frenchtown) · [KHS marker #508: Remember the Raisin](https://history.ky.gov/markers/remember-the-raisin)

### 🏺 The C. and O. Mounds at Paintsville
On the Levisa Fork at Paintsville, the C. and O. Mounds are two Adena burial mounds that William S. Webb dug and used to explain how Adena people lived.
Source: [tDAR: Webb 1942, The C. and O. Mounds at Paintsville](https://core.tdar.org/document/176231/the-c-and-o-mounds-at-paintsville-sites-jo-2-and-jo-9-johnson-county-kentucky) · [R. Berle Clay 1998, The Essential Features of Adena Ritual (PDF)](https://portola.press/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/clay-1998-adena-ritual.pdf)

### 🛣️ The Maysville Road and Jackson’s veto, 1830
The Maysville Road from the Ohio to Lexington drew Andrew Jackson’s 1830 veto of federal aid, yet Kentucky finished the turnpike in 1835 on today’s US 68.
Source: [Grokipedia: Maysville Road veto](https://grokipedia.com/page/Maysville_Road_veto) · [KHS marker #144: Maysville Road](https://history.ky.gov/markers/maysville-road)
