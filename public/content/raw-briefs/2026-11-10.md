# Col. James Smith, Princeton, Middlesboro & the Westo

*Kentucky History Morning Update — Tuesday, November 10, 2026*

🧭 **Col. James Smith, captive, scout and Cane Ridge elder**
James Smith was captured in Pennsylvania in 1755 and adopted into a Kahnawake Mohawk family before escaping in 1759. In 1766 he explored the Cumberland and Tennessee country to the mouth of the Tennessee. In 1788 he settled near Cane Ridge in Bourbon County, where he served in the 1799 constitutional convention and the legislature and was an elder of the Cane Ridge church. John Bradford printed his captivity narrative in Lexington in 1799.

🏛️ **Princeton grows from the Big Spring at Eddy Grove**
In 1797 Capt. William Prince explored Eddy Grove and found the Big Spring, and in 1798 he built Shandy Hall above it. Caldwell County was formed in 1809. After Prince died in 1810, his widow gave land for the courthouse. Princetown became the county seat in 1817, was renamed Princeton the next year, and was incorporated in 1820.

☄️ **Middlesboro, a town built inside a meteor crater**
Middlesboro sits in a round basin about three miles across, left by an impact roughly 300 million years ago. Shatter cones found in 1966 confirmed the crater. Alexander A. Arthur's American Association began the town in 1886, and the city was built in the basin in 1889.

🗺️ **The Westo, Erie refugees who carried the Beaver Wars south**
By 1656 newcomers whom most historians identify as Erie refugees from the Beaver Wars had settled at the falls of the James River in Virginia. As the Westo, they moved to the Savannah River and raided for the Virginia and Carolina slave trade. In 1680 Carolina traders armed the Savannahs, a Shawnee band, and the Westo War broke Westo power within a few years. These events took place outside Kentucky, so this story has no Kentucky map pin.
