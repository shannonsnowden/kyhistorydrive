🤠 William Christian of Beargrass
Virginia militia colonel, Patrick Henry’s brother-in-law, and signer of the Fincastle Resolutions, Christian moved his family to Jefferson County in 1785 and directed defense of the Louisville settlements from Fort William on the Middle Fork of Beargrass (the older A’Sturgus Station of 1779). On April 9, 1786, leading a pursuit north of the Ohio, he was killed near present-day Jeffersonville, Indiana; Christian County was later named for him.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Christian_(Virginia)

🏙️ Versailles Takes Shape
On June 23, 1792—weeks after Kentucky’s statehood—the legislature seated Woodford County’s courts on about 80–100 acres owned by the infant Hezekiah Briscoe. His guardian, Marquis Calmes, named the town for Versailles, France, in honor of Lafayette, a family friend. The Bluegrass county seat still pronounces it vər-SAYLZ.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles,_Kentucky

🪶 Miami Hunters Return North of the Ohio
Iroquois pressure in the Beaver Wars emptied much of the Ohio Valley by the late 1600s. By the mid-1700s the Miami—and Shawnee and other Wabash–Maumee peoples—had pushed hunting territories back into northern Kentucky, using the Licking–Ohio corridor for game and salt long after permanent villages had gone.
https://nkytribune.com/2020/11/our-rich-history-newport-american-indian-overview-local-tribes-left-the-region-by-the-late-1600s/

🏛️ Robbins Mounds, Boone County
Two Middle Woodland Adena burial mounds (15Be3 and 15Be14) once crowned an upland ridge near the Ohio. University of Kentucky crews excavated them in 1939: the larger stood about 20 feet high and 131 feet across, beginning as a 30-foot circular screen around ritual fires before growing through decades of log-and-bark tombs holding at least a hundred people.
https://archaeology.ky.gov/Find-a-Site/Pages/Robbins-Mound-Complex.aspx

🥃 Basil Hayden’s High-Rye Trail
In 1785 Maryland Catholic Basil Hayden Sr. led about twenty-five families to Pottinger’s Creek in what became Nelson County. He distilled with a heavier rye share than many corn-heavy neighbors, donated land for Holy Cross (Kentucky’s first Catholic church), and later inspired his grandson’s Old Grand-Dad label—and today’s Basil Hayden’s small-batch line.
