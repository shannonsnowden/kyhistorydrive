🤠 George Rogers Clark
Virginia surveyor-turned-militia leader George Rogers Clark staged at Corn Island in the Falls of the Ohio in 1778, then launched the Illinois Campaign that took Kaskaskia and Vincennes from the British. The settlement he left behind became Louisville, chartered in 1780 and named for France’s Louis XVI. Fort Nelson later rose on the mainland as his western headquarters.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Rogers_Clark

🏙️ Danville and Constitution Square
After the Kentucky District court moved from Harrodsburg to Crow’s Station, Danville’s public square (laid out 1785) hosted ten conventions from 1784 to 1792. Delegates there framed the first state constitution in April 1792, clearing the way for Kentucky to enter the Union as the fifteenth state on June 1. Replicas of the log courthouse, jail, and meetinghouse still mark the ground.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Square_Historic_Site

⚔️ Battle of Blue Licks
On August 19, 1782, about 182 Kentucky militia chasing Captain William Caldwell’s raiders walked into an ambush on a Licking River ridge in today’s Robertson County. Roughly 50 Butler’s Rangers and 200–300 Shawnee, Delaware, and Wyandot warriors fired from ravines and bluffs; Todd, Trigg, and Boone’s son Israel were among the dead. It was the settlers’ bloodiest Revolutionary defeat in Kentucky, months after Yorktown.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blue_Licks

🏛️ Mount Horeb Earthworks
Above Elkhorn Creek near Lexington, Adena people built a circular ditch-and-embankment enclosure more than 2,000 years ago for periodic ritual gatherings. WPA digs under William S. Webb in 1939 found a ring of large paired posts that likely screened ceremonies before the earthen walls went up. The University of Kentucky now protects a partially reconstructed site as Adena Park.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Horeb_Earthworks_Complex

🦇 Great Saltpetre Cave
South of Mount Vernon in Rockcastle County, Great Saltpetre Cave was mined hard for calcium nitrate during the War of 1812, when British blockades cut off imported saltpeter for gunpowder. Dozens of workers (many enslaved) leached cave earth in wooden vats and shipped crystals to powder mills until trade reopened after 1815. The Rockcastle Karst Conservancy keeps the preserve today.
