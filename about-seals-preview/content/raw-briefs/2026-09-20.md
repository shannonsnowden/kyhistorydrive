🤠 Capt. Jack Jouett
On the night of June 3–4, 1781, Virginia militia captain John "Jack" Jouett Jr. spotted Banastre Tarleton's British cavalry near Cuckoo Tavern and rode about forty miles of backwoods trails to warn Thomas Jefferson at Monticello and the Virginia Assembly at Charlottesville. Most leaders escaped; Virginia later awarded him a sword and pistols. In 1782 he moved west, married Sally Robards, backed Kentucky statehood, served in the Kentucky House for Mercer and then Woodford, and in 1797 built the brick house still standing on Craig's Creek near Versailles—farming, milling, and distilling until his death in Bath County in 1822.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Jouett

🏙 Shepherdsville on Salt River
On December 11, 1793, the young Kentucky General Assembly established a town on fifty acres of Adam Shepherd's land on the north side of Salt River, at the falls where the great road to Bullitt's Lick crossed. Shepherd—born Adam Schaeffer in Pennsylvania in 1757, a surveyor who came west about 1781—laid out the lots; trustees sold them for money or country produce. When Bullitt County was organized in 1796–97, Shepherdsville became the seat, anchoring settlement between the Wilderness Road corridor and the Falls of the Ohio salt trade.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherdsville,_Kentucky

🪶 Shawnee Chillicothe divisions
The Shawnee were a loose confederacy of five divisions—Chalahgawtha (Chillicothe), Mekoche, Kispoko, Pekowi, and Hathawekela—each with traditional roles; Chillicothe customarily supplied political leadership. The principal Chillicothe village moved when war or settlement forced it, so several "Chillicothes" appear in the record. From Old Chillicothe on the Little Miami (near present Xenia, Ohio), war chief Blackfish's people staged raids into Kentucky to contest the hunting grounds; Boone was held there in 1778, and Kentucky militia under Clark burned the town in 1780 after Bird's invasion.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalahgawtha

🏺 Hansen Site (15GP14)
Near South Portsmouth in Greenup County, the Hansen Site sits on an Ohio River terrace just upstream from Lower Shawneetown and across from the Scioto mouth. Excavations document Late Archaic summer camps around 2000 BCE (Cave Run, Merom, and Rowlette points), then denser Middle–Late Woodland communities (about 300–600 CE) with circular post structures, tool workshops, and storage. Fort Ancient people used the terrace later. The spot ties northeastern Kentucky archaeology to the same river mouth that later hosted Shawnee towns and the Portsmouth Earthworks complex.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansen_site

🪨 Natural Bridge sandstone arch
In Powell and Wolfe counties, a 78-foot-long, 65-foot-high arch of Corbin Sandstone (Breathitt Formation, Middle Pennsylvanian) spans a gorge on the Middle Fork of the Red River. Quartz-rich river sands capped softer Mississippian limestones; Alleghenian uplift and millions of years of freeze–thaw and stream cutting carved the arch. Long a landmark for Indigenous hunters and settlers, the site became a railroad excursion stop in the 1890s and one of Kentucky's original state parks in 1926, beside Red River Gorge and the Daniel Boone National Forest.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Bridge_State_Resort_Park
