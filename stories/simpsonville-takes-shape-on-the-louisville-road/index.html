Shelby County · Early commonwealth · 1794 – 1833

Simpsonville takes shape on the Louisville road

In Shelby County, Isaac Watkins petitioned to lay out Simpsonville in 1816 as a waystation on the Louisville–Shelbyville road.

Photos

Bank of Simpsonville Building (1902) (1902)

Source: Publichall · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0

A post office opened in 1821; the town incorporated in 1833. It was named for Capt. John Simpson, a Shelby County lawyer and Speaker of the Kentucky House (1810–11) who fought at Fallen Timbers (1794), won a seat in Congress in 1812, then died at the River Raisin on January 22, 1813.

By the mid-1820s the place was a busy Midland Trail stage stop — home ground for anyone driving US 60 past Bagdad today.
