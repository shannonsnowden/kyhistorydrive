Susquehannock and the Ohio Valley Door

The Susquehannock (also Andaste / Minqua / later Conestoga) were an Iroquoian people of the lower Susquehanna who dominated mid-Atlantic fur trade in the early 1600s and fought the Haudenosaunee through the Beaver Wars.

Photos

Susquehannock artifacts on display, State Museum of Pennsylvania (Harrisburg) — Susquehannock homeland, not a Kentucky site (2007)

Source: Ruhrfisch (US Census) · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0

After Iroquois conquest around 1675–76, their power collapsed and survivors scattered or were absorbed. That hinge helped empty and reopen the Ohio Valley as contested “middle ground”—the hunting country Shawnee, Mingo, and others later claimed, and the same corridor Virginia traders and long hunters followed into Kentucky.

Their story is not a Kentucky village tale, but one of the eastern Beaver Wars shocks that remade who could hunt south of the Ohio.
