Erie — the Cat Nation cleared from the lake shore

The Erie (Erielhénon, “people of the long tail”), called la Nation du Chat by the French, were an Iroquoian people of fortified maize villages along southern Lake Erie.

Photos

Erie / Cat Nation territory (historical map)

Source: Wikimedia Commons

In the Beaver Wars they sheltered Huron refugees and clashed with the Seneca; between about 1654 and 1656 Iroquois armies with Dutch firearms destroyed their towns. Survivors were adopted into Iroquois nations or scattered south and west.

That collapse helped open the upper Ohio Valley as contested hunting ground — the same middle ground later claimed by Shawnee, Mingo, and Wyandot parties who wintered and raided south of the river into Kentucky.
